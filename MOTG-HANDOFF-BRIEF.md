## What MOTG is
- An independent supporter / booster brand for the **LeMoyne-Owen College** golf team (HBCU in Memphis, TN, NCAA Division II / SIAC), created by head golf coach **Dominique Worthen**.
- Golf-wizard brand. Colors: purple, gold, cream. All artwork (the "Magicians on the Green" wordmark, the wizard character, the M logomark) was created by the coach himself.
- Sells merch (hats, apparel, "Magic Stick" leather headcovers) and points supporters toward donating to the college. There is also a "Magic on the Greens" golf tournament (Sept 13 to 15, 2026).
- A Shopify store is already built and is currently **password-locked (not public yet)**. A pre-launch email-capture landing page is live behind that lock.

## The money model (DECIDED)
- **Merch purchases** flow to the venture (the coach's business, an LLC to be formed).
- **Donations** flow directly to the college's official giving page. They are tax-deductible, the college controls the funds, and MOTG never holds donated money.
- **Give-back to the golf program: 10% of the merch sale price + a guaranteed $1,000 per year minimum.** If 10% of sales totals under $1,000 in a year, MOTG covers the difference.
- Honest public line: **"10% of every purchase supports LeMoyne-Owen golf."** NOT "100% to the team," because the coach keeps the operating margin (this is a for-profit supporter brand, not a pure pass-through).

## Legal / structure status
- **Nothing is in writing with the college yet.** Only a verbal conversation with a college representative.
- **No LLC formed yet.** The plan is to form one.
- A one-page **Terms of Engagement term sheet** has been drafted for the coach to bring to the college's Athletics and Advancement offices. It covers: license to use the "Magicians" name and marks, what the team gets (the 10% + $1k), the coach's conflict-of-interest disclosure, NIL guardrails, independence and liability, term and exit, and signature lines.
- Guidance given: get **written college sign-off BEFORE going public**; form the LLC; keep donations 100% through the college; keep merch language honest; have a Memphis attorney finalize the paperwork.
- **Risk flags surfaced:** "Magicians" plus purple/gold is the college's athletic identity, so drawing his own logos does NOT clear trademark / false-affiliation risk; the coach is a college employee, so this triggers conflict-of-interest / outside-employment policy; NIL rules apply if any player likeness is used; possible charitable-solicitation registration; truth-in-advertising on any give-back claim.

## The bigger opportunity (UNDER DISCUSSION, not decided)
- Idea: turn MOTG into a **fundraising + merch platform for the entire athletic department** (all sports), with golf as the pilot to prove the model.
- Important shift: this flips the coach from volunteer booster into a **paid vendor selling to his own employer**, a larger conflict of interest that must be handled in the open. Recommended: the LLC is the vendor, disclose it in writing, route it through the AD and business office, and the coach recuses from the college's side of the decision. If the conflict is too hot, bring in a **co-owner who is not a college employee** to front the vendor relationship.

## Recommended pricing to the department (DRAFT)
Philosophy: "Free to start, we earn as you earn." Near-zero upfront so a cash-tight D-II department can say yes.
- **Merch (the engine):** $0 setup for the pilot and first cohort. Each team gets a **15% to 20% royalty** of every sale. MOTG keeps the margin after product cost and the team royalty.
  - Example on a $30 hat: about $11 product + $1 fees, about $5.40 to the team (18%), about $12.60 to MOTG.
- **Donations:** NEVER take a percentage of gifts (fundraising-ethics and paid-solicitor problem). Charge a flat fee instead: $99 to $199 per month per team, or a department bundle at $500 to $1,000 per month, or $500 to $1,500 flat per major campaign.
- **Later teams:** $250 to $500 one-time setup.
- **Cost tip that makes the margin work:** run every team as a collection inside ONE Shopify store ($39/month total, not per team) and use print-on-demand so there is no inventory risk.

## Open blind spots to resolve BEFORE pitching the department
**Three go/no-go items (find answers first):**
1. Who already controls the college's trademarks / licensing? (A licensing agent like CLC/Learfield, or an exclusive bookstore/spirit-wear vendor.) If someone else holds the marks, the platform cannot sell marked merch without their sign-off.
2. Does this require a formal procurement or bid process? (Employee-as-vendor conflict; federal-funds rules if any federal money touches athletics.)
3. Real capacity to deliver at scale while coaching full-time.

**Also on the radar:**
- Paid-fundraiser / solicitor registration (Tennessee) once charging to run donation campaigns.
- Sales tax registration and a Memphis/Shelby business license as merch scales across states.
- NIL rule: generic team/logo merch only, no individual player name/number/likeness on product without a compliance-cleared NIL deal.
- Donor-data ownership: advancement will not hand a private vendor its donor list. Settle who owns customer vs. donor data up front.
- Financial transparency IS the product: separate LLC bank account, clean books, monthly per-team statements. It builds trust and it is the coach's defense if ever accused of skimming.
- Golf's give-back looks like self-dealing once the coach is the paid vendor. Put golf on the same standard deal as the other teams, or disclose why not.
- The golf pilot will flatter the numbers (it is the coach's own sport). Do not let the AD extrapolate straight across.
- Protect against the college taking it in-house after it works: contract term, termination-with-notice plus a transition/buyout, and owning the systems and original art.
- Insurance beyond the LLC: product liability, general liability, event coverage.

Reframe: handled well, each of these becomes a **selling point** in the pitch (transparency, compliance, insurance, you own your data, clean exit).
